---
name: Commercial assessment positioning
description: Owner-approved business positioning and boundaries for the public assessment website.
---

The website sells independent MCP security assessments. Distinguish the free PyPI scanner from paid independent verification. Preserve existing functionality; do not rebuild the site to make this commercial change.

**Why:** The owner explicitly requested a credible assessment business website, without invented testimonials, certifications, vulnerability discoveries, or assessment coverage.

**How to apply:** Keep public claims tied to authentic or explicitly synthetic evidence. Do not present an automated scanner export as independent verification. New payment processing requires separate approval.

Make the landing page assessment-first, not workspace- or recurring-plan-first. Remove public monthly offers, the weekly re-checks checkout link, and compliance-score selling points. Keep workspace sign-in, technical names, scan API, scanning logic and dashboard unchanged.

**Why:** The owner's later landing-page rewrite explicitly superseded the earlier product-first and recurring-offer direction.

**How to apply:** Sell single-server and full assessments as scoped engagements, each with one written report and one retest. Do not promise a call, walkthrough, meeting, booking or calendar step in either package. Preserve existing account/scanning/history behavior and never add inquiry storage to findings tables. Do not republish production automatically.

Replace the public synthetic sample with the owner-supplied real report. Do not publish the synthetic demonstration alongside it or leave placeholder samples linked anywhere on the site.

**Why:** The owner explicitly rejected keeping synthetic evidence on a site whose argument is that evidence can be checked.

**How to apply:** Keep all sample links pointed at the real report and retire the former synthetic route with a redirect. Preserving supplied claims is not independent verification of them. Never invent evidence or hash characters.

Freeze the named-client sample's wording. Do not edit it later for tone without the owner's review.

**Why:** The owner stated that the report names a client who gave permission and explicitly asked to freeze the wording.

**How to apply:** Enforce the approved-copy regression fixture. Only change the copy or that fixture following a specific owner-approved correction. The owner supplied the complete severity basis and hash-header wording; the earlier truncation was caused by the chat client, not by the report.

Use the written-scope form as the assessment offer's primary action. Submit opens a prefilled mailto draft to the existing assessment inbox; it does not call status, save online, or send server-side. Keep Resend connected but unused by this form.

**Why:** The owner explicitly replaced server-side delivery with the visitor's email client so a status or sending-service failure cannot block the request. Invoicing follows agreed scope; submission never authorizes testing.

**How to apply:** Explain that the visitor must press Send in their email client. Do not claim saving, delivery or receipt from opening a draft. Do not require a verified Resend sender for this mailto-only path. Keep unrelated inquiry-page wording and existing functionality unchanged unless requested.

The scope-form fallback must remain a direct mailto link to the same assessment inbox, preserving the visitor's entered scope rather than navigating to another intake page.

**Why:** The owner requested a usable email fallback when online status or submission fails, without silently losing the request.

**How to apply:** URI-encode draft answers, preserve fields on failure, and explain that opening a draft does not send it. Report a real test email's delivery evidence separately from mocked tests or provider acceptance.

The landing scope form asks what buyer or review is waiting on the assessment and offers an optional permission checkbox for publishing a redacted result.

**Why:** The owner explicitly requested these questions; publication permission must not be assumed from a request for testing.

**How to apply:** Include both answers in the saved inquiry and notification. Keep publication consent separate from testing authority and never preselect it.

Use the verified custom domain as the primary public site while preserving the existing Replit hostname where safe. Trust only exact approved origins; do not use wildcard origins or weaken authentication/CSRF checks.

**Why:** The owner requested full custom-domain support without breaking existing access or changing DNS, customer data, scanner behavior, or billing.

**How to apply:** Keep canonical metadata, generated links, production public URL and authentication origin checks consistent. Do not assume rendering the sign-in form proves OAuth completion or authenticated actions work.
